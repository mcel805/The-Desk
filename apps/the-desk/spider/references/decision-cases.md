# Chart-grounded decision cases

Read these when an entry family, pool choice, cancellation or stop decision is ambiguous. They summarize twelve retained teaching examples; they are not unseen-chart forecasts, a backtest or proof of fills. C08–C12 incorporate the resumed visual pass across the remaining 45 Videos-tab uploads.

## C01 — When a swept extreme becomes a new pool

[Source V008 04:36–06:18](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=9URArIuZ2BM&t=276s).

The diagram draws an upper sweep and departure, a later return that respects the upper region, and another departure. The later respect changes the story around that original high.

**Decision:** Do not target a fresh sweep-origin extreme solely because it is the highest point. Wait for evidence of participation/rebuilding, or use another supported objective.

**Next evidence:** A later respect-and-departure sequence may qualify that old extreme as a new pool.

**Limit:** The diagram explains the presenter’s hypothesis. It cannot prove which orders exist or that all fresh extremes are empty.

## C02 — The market determines the stop distance

[Source V012 04:19–04:50](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jGAS_1bOevA&t=259s).

The chart shows a direct long tool below a recently respected buyer region, with the stop boundary beyond the prior left-side sweep low.

**Decision:** Keep the true origin low as the stop anchor. If the distance is unattractive, use a different valid entry or pass rather than tightening the stop into the region.

**Next evidence:** Clearance of the chosen buyer pool activates the direct entry; its observed contact and a realistic fill remain separate questions.

**Limit:** This single retrospective frame cannot reconstruct exact fill prices, spread or intrabar stop/target order.

## C04 — A new local trap can replace a mechanical deepest-low requirement

[Source V059 03:28–06:47](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=E2n7KMQDYIU&t=208s).

The source diagram retains a deep older origin on the left, while a newer sequence on the right induces sellers, then buyers, around a higher internal region. The illustrated later low clears that active region while staying above the oldest low.

**Decision:** Follow the newly formed participation and actual local trap. Do not require a distant old extreme merely because it began a previous BOS leg.

**Next evidence:** Require the selected setup’s actual pool run, usable block and live upper objective; a local high break by itself is not enough.

**Limit:** This is a diagram, not a verified trade or quantified probability. The older low remains a competing/future reference.

## C05 — Stop chasing the next old pool after the relevant trap

[Source V060 07:25–08:31](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=LWEuNvtjQNA&t=445s).

Paired diagrams show the reversal after a relevant local trap, with the text “No more sells” under the bullish case and an older boundary still present.

**Decision:** Default against fresh shorts whose only reason is the next deeper historical low after those relevant buyers have been trapped. Mirror the filter for fresh longs after seller trapping.

**Next evidence:** Rebuild the active sequence, wait for a supported continuation, or stay out if the older pool still makes the immediate path unclear.

**Limit:** This applies to fresh entries in the selected sequence. It is not a command to close every existing position, reverse automatically or ignore larger-timeframe conflict.

## C07 — Pass a valid location when its genuine stop fails the chosen R filter

[Source V064 07:10–09:00](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=9gUKOkgODK4&t=430s).

The 30m YM chart shows a broad upper blue region and a short tool covering the whole origin. The tool’s displayed reward/risk is about 2.27; the narration calls it about 2.2 and discusses a personal 3R minimum.

**Decision:** The presenter passes the broad entry under his own threshold. A deeper valid entry with the same stop or a new smaller confirmation block are alternatives. A stop arbitrarily inside the origin is not.

**Next evidence:** Wait for the chosen valid deeper entry or a genuinely qualified smaller setup. Never move the target to an arbitrary R just to improve the display.

**Limit:** The threshold is the presenter’s preference, not Mark’s setting. The screenshot is a replay drawing and does not establish executable size, realized P&L or edge.

## C08 — Correct direction can still have no usable entry

**Next evidence:** A new local sweep-origin, departure, secondary pool and run, with an intact lower objective and viable true stop.

**Limit:** The historical selloff supports the directional example, not a feasible fill or proof that the move could be traded under the original cutoff.

## C11 — Refinement is optional; an arbitrary tight stop is not refinement

[Source V034 05:12–05:56](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=lmGJ2OxBzP0&t=312s). The inspected 05:21 frame is a 30-minute gold chart with a long tool beginning beneath the nearer low and a stop below its chosen origin.

**Decision:** A readable higher-frame setup can supply the entry directly. Use a smaller frame only when it provides its own qualified block/pool/trigger and a justified smaller stop.

**Next evidence:** The selected pool run into the entry region, or a genuine new smaller confirmation setup if the broad stop is unattractive.

**Limit:** A broad frame and its lower-frame view are not independent confirmations. The example does not establish performance parity across timeframes.

## C12 — Partial size is part of the selected plan

[Source V062 08:14–08:51](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=HfnKU1D_woM&t=494s). The frame at 08:16 shows an upper short tool, an intermediate lower region, a farther lower objective, and a later retracement. The narration specifies a 25% partial in this example; V042 uses 75% and reports an unfinished final runner.

**Decision:** Select the management pool and fraction before the attempt; record whether fractions use original or remaining size. Do not copy one video's percentage into every setup.

**Next evidence:** The chosen technical management event, followed by the specified partial/stop action and continued validity or failure of the runner thesis.

**Limit:** The tool cannot verify actual size or returns. A remaining target does not cancel a failed child stop, and unrealized runner R is not realized profit.

## Scope and trigger evidence

For every induced/trapped label, retain its timeframe, sequence ID and relationship to the parent thesis. A local trap does not by itself reverse the entire higher-timeframe map. If the new-entry filter and the parent draw conflict and the sources do not settle the immediate path, report the conflict and wait for a fresh setup. Do not solve the conflict by erasing an old level or treating the last BOS as global direction.

A source arrow, risk/reward box, trade-tool label or filename containing filled does not establish execution. Separate planned price, observed pool run, source-reported trade, hypothetical paper fill and verified fill. Never promote retrospective image evidence into a cutoff-safe forecast.
