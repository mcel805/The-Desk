# Elliott Wave analysis

## Method identity

Keep three lenses separate: traditional Frost/Prechter Elliott Wave, Neely’s NEoWave, and an explicitly named house interpretation. Do not merge rules to rescue a count. The existing `agents/cleo/CURRICULUM.md`, `REFERENCE-LIBRARY.md`, and `PREMARKET-BASELINE.md` retain the longer study plan and detailed visual grammar.

## Count protocol

- Start from the largest supplied timeframe and retain lower charts as refinements, not replacements. Record timeframe, data source, cutoff, scale, contract/roll convention and complete/partial bars.
- Identify pivots before labels. Give the degree and parent path; timeframe alone does not define degree.
- Apply sourced hard rules before guidelines. Under traditional impulse rules, check wave-2 origin, wave-3 length relative to waves 1 and 5, and wave-4 overlap with wave 1, while checking pattern-specific exceptions such as diagonals separately.
- Keep one primary and one materially distinct alternate only when evidence supports them. Name unresolved subdivisions instead of inventing detail.
- Distinguish observed history from projected legs. State trigger type, invalidation, conditional target, and horizon. A rule violation invalidates the candidate under that method; a guideline is not a hard rule.
- Measure fib relationships only from stated pivots and show the calculation. Ratios are descriptive relationships; convergence is not a calibrated probability.
- Freeze dated forecasts before outcome data is visible; retain revisions as new records. Score structural rule adherence separately from directional outcome.

## Presentation

Place labels at observed pivots, maintain nested identity across timeframes, keep alternate and forecast layers visually distinct, and include source/method/version. Preserve the original chart. If the source/locator for a method rule is unavailable, say it is not verified from source.
