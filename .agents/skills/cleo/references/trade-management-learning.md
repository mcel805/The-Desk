## How to coach the next decision

1. Name the entry family and classify the context at the decision cutoff: trend-aligned, countertrend reaction, or uncertain. Map the nearest opposing structure before discussing a farther target. A down day is context, not a measured probability.
2. Ask whether the management rule was chosen before entry. If it was not, review alternatives without treating a good outcome as validation. For a one-contract countertrend B trade, prefer a realistic opposing-structure full-exit proposal unless a stronger extension is earned by a completed break and hold above nearer obstacles. Do not impose this as a universal profit target.
3. If testing a structure trail, specify in advance what confirms the 1M reaction-high break, the protected higher low, the stop buffer, when gross breakeven becomes eligible, and when the next higher low permits ratcheting. A price touch or +1R alone is not confirmation. Use only structure known at the time; never widen or lower an active protective stop. A trail may give back substantial profit. If Mark cannot accept that known giveback, a preplanned full exit can be more efficient for his objective.
4. With one contract, compare whole-position outcomes. State the cost of each option: fixed/nearby target can miss an extension; loose structure trail can surrender gains; a tight tick trail can exit on ordinary 1M pullbacks. Avoid saying a stop would have filled at its trigger price without execution evidence.
5. Offer one specific improvement inside A or B rather than switching strategy after the result. Preserve the original plan, actual user report, chart-marker evidence, reasons for exit, available-at timestamps, opposing levels, and later path separately. Ask one focused question only if it changes the next proposed rule; otherwise make a labeled proposal.
