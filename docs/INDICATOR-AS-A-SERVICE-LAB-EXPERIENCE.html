First pilot · Liquidity

Freeze the question before the first outcome

The proposal below starts with external-zone first touch because the current engine already records confirmation and touch. It is a pilot cohort, not yet the all-signal score. Final target, invalidation, and horizon require a Scientist-signed protocol.

proposedEligible opportunity

One newly confirmed external BSL or SSL zone on a specified dated MYM contract and 5m chart. Follow it from its actual confirmation bar, never from the earlier pivot bar. Log every eligible zone, including no-touch and missing-data cases.

proposedTrigger

First closed 5m bar after confirmation that intersects the zone. Freeze the zone ID, side, boundaries, prior touches, source, bar cutoff, engine/config hash, and known context at that close. Do not reclassify a failed signal later.

must freezeFuture outcome

After the trigger closes, did price make a predefined favorable rejection before a predefined adverse boundary within a fixed number of closed bars? Timeout and same-bar target/failure ambiguity need explicit treatment. This measures zone reaction, not a trade fill or net P&L.

must freezeComparison

Measure the existing Liquidity version first. Then compare a candidate on the same opportunity set and same future windows. Track how many signals each version emits, so filtering away hard cases cannot masquerade as improvement.

Two probabilities, if both are wanted: chance an eligible zone is touched within a horizon, and chance of a favorable reaction after first touch. The Tool Belt headline should name one primary probability; the other remains a separate diagnostic. The earlier MYM forward Lab concept also separates these questions.
Scientific cycle

What the Lab does each day

Development, locked evaluation, and untouched forward sessions have different jobs.

01

Register

Chief Scientist freezes the signal, outcome, eligible cohort, exclusions, minimum useful effect, sample and uncertainty plan, baseline, and guardrails.

02

Qualify input

Confirm source rights, contract/calendar, timezone, closed-bar completeness, missing-data policy, and retained raw-input hash. Stop if source identity or rights are unclear.

03

Replay causally

Build a baseline on development data; test replay/reload parity and look-ahead. Keep selection data out of the locked evaluation. Separate overlapping event windows.

04

Observe forward

Record every eligible signal or abstention before the future path is known. Resolve each as success, failure, timeout, ambiguous, or unavailable under the frozen rule.

05

Score and challenge

Publish counts, coverage, outcome rate with interval, candidate minus incumbent, session/regime splits, and defects. Independent QA reproduces numerator and denominator.

06

Decide

Retain, advance, investigate, quarantine, or revert. Only a reviewed result may update the card; rule edits start a new version and new forward evaluation.

Evidence standard

What counts as a significant enhancement?

“Proven” here means a reproducible result under a frozen test with uncertainty and limitations reported. Future performance is never guaranteed.

Primary effect

Predeclare the minimum useful increase in reaction reliability versus the incumbent on the same opportunity cohort. Report the paired difference and a session-cluster uncertainty interval. Plan sample size from that effect and observed clustering; no arbitrary “100 signals is enough” rule.

Guardrails

Coverage, signal count, false positives, detection delay, missing-data rate, stability, and subgroup harm must stay inside frozen limits. A 70% hit rate on only a tiny sliver of eligible zones is not automatically better.

Probability quality

If the indicator later emits per-signal probabilities, test calibration by score bucket and Brier score against a simple base-rate forecast. A raw success fraction alone does not validate a 70% forecast for each signal.

Promotion gate: source and labels pass QA; the predeclared effect and uncertainty rule pass; guardrails hold; untouched forward evidence persists across sessions; an independent reviewer can reproduce the score; human chart validation and rollback evidence are recorded. Each failed or inconclusive candidate remains in the ledger.
Why sample size matters

The same 60% can mean very different things

Illustration only: a Wilson 95% interval for independent binary events. Real liquidity signals cluster, so the Lab must also account for session and overlapping-zone dependence.

What it teaches

The point estimate stays 60%. More independent evidence narrows uncertainty. Repeated signals during one market move are less informative than the same number spread across independent sessions. A positive point estimate without a predeclared comparison does not establish an improvement.

Then test one change

Use development observations to propose a target or stop rule. Replay baseline and candidate on the same eligible opportunities with the same entry and future path; mark unresolved intrabar ordering. Experiment contract

Every Lab question follows one reusable structure

The Lab helps draft this in conversation; the Scientist owns the frozen method and an independent reviewer checks the result.

Question and unit

What decision is being studied? Define one eligible signal, plan, session, or paper trial as the observation unit. Keep target/stop economics separate from the indicator’s probability score.

Baseline and candidate

Specify the accepted version, planned parameters, comparison rule, and exactly one candidate change. Retain rejected and inconclusive ideas in the ledger.

Evidence and promotion

Freeze source rights, cutoff, outcome, costs, exclusions, primary metric, sample/interval plan, and guardrails. A measured average is descriptive; a significant gain needs an untouched forward comparison and review.

Method references

Why these checks are here

  • NIST: confidence intervals for proportions supports a Wilson interval for a binary event rate.
  • NIST: confidence limits for a mean supports reporting uncertainty around measured averages.
  • scikit-learn: probability calibration distinguishes reliability curves and Brier scoring from classification accuracy.
  • scikit-learn: time-ordered evaluation explains why future observations must not enter earlier model selection.
  • NIST: multiple comparisons motivates logging every candidate and controlling repeated searches for a favorable rule.
