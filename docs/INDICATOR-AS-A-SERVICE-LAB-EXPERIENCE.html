How to use the Lab

One question. Five steps.

Keep the question and success measure clear before the test begins.

01

Ask

“Does this Liquidity signal work better with a different target or stop?”

02

Baseline

Measure the current rule on a defined source, contract, timeframe, and cohort.

03

Change one thing

Write down the candidate rule and what result would count as improvement.

04

Test forward

Freeze the rule, record every eligible case, and wait for future outcomes.

05

Review

Check counts, uncertainty, coverage, costs, and data quality. Keep, revise, or reject.

Before the test

Choose the source, eligible cases, timeframe, outcome, and observation window. Record the current version as the baseline.

After the test

Compare baseline and candidate on the same cases. Show unresolved and ambiguous outcomes; do not tune on the test results.

How the result is used

Evidence before the score changes.

The score must say what event was measured and which observations it describes.

Indicator score

Liquidity pilot results stay labeled as a pilot until the included signal families have a shared outcome and reviewed evidence. Show rate, interval, sample count, and coverage.

Experiment decision

The Scientist freezes the test. The Lab calculates it. A Lab tester and independent reviewer check the result before a human considers a change.

Paper economics

Target/stop results stay in their own report. They do not automatically change the signal probability score. All experiments remain research-only.
